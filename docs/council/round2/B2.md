# B2 (A3 + U1 + V2): 5 roles, per-tester sandbox, leak tracing

Role-played lens.

## (a) Strongest case
- **Sandbox is what makes the tests worth anything.** 8 attackers work alone. One person cannot form 3-of-5 without playing several roles. A per-tester sandbox (all 5 accounts pre-made, reset button) is the only honest way to let them reach release, refusal, tamper and trace (V2 S2-S6). Without it the week is spent on "how do I log in" tickets, which is B1's own stated worst case.
- **Tracing is the one feature a textbook cannot supply.** `logic.mjs` already has `copyFor`, `visibleFingerprint`, `traceSeats`; the order, not the printed ID, is the fingerprint. Marginal cost is a small matcher and one screen, and it is the Part B demo moment.
- **Five roles is the minimum that makes the security claims testable.** Setter-vs-setter confidentiality (S3), "auditor cannot read questions" and separation of duties exist only if those roles exist. Seeded roles with no screens are untestable claims.

## (b) Best attacks on B1 (3 roles)
1. **Three roles cannot demonstrate the headline claims.** Without a setter, "setter B cannot open A's question" cannot be attacked. Without an auditor, log verification and tracing have no actor, so Part B has no leak story. B1's "seed or script them" means the tester cannot reach those paths at all. P1 also still demands a lone tester to complete 3-of-5, but offers no sandbox: that gap is where "just create five accounts" quietly reappears.
2. **B1's "reset button + documented test account" concedes our sandbox.** A2 section (c).4 asks for the same thing. The only dispute is how many accounts it holds, and a seeded account costs about the same as a login row.

## (c) Concession
B1 is right that breadth kills schedules. Setter, custodian and auditor screens must be thin (one card or table each), and U1's user-management UI, reminders, and 4-of-5 fallback UI are cut. Also, `traceSeats` currently requires an exact fingerprint match on a fixed prefix. A cropped photo needs windowed matching, so tracing is not free, and 3-question fragments across 60 seats may be ambiguous on top of the 32-bit PRNG (to be fixed).

## (d) Compromise
**3 full screens, 2 thin ones, one sandbox.**
- Full: admin, custodian (approval card), superintendent (gate checklist). Thin, read-only-ish: setter (one add-question form), auditor (verify chain + trace box).
- Per-tester sandbox: one seeded exam per login, five pre-created users, "reset my exam" button, and a "play all roles" switcher that is labelled a demo artefact in the UI and the guide.
- Trace: windowed matcher, ranked candidates, human Confirm/Dismiss, 3-question test.
- Cut: user admin UI, fallback UI, reminders, any role beyond five.

Cost: about 4 builder-days of AI-paired work on the 12-15 Nov calendar. Relative to B1's 3-role plan: +0.5 day setter/auditor shells, +0.5 day sandbox seed and reset, +0.5 day windowed trace. Roughly +1.5 days. Day order: auth/roles/deploy first, then pipeline, sandbox, and last trace; if 15 Nov noon arrives without trace working, cut to a scripted trace and say so.

## (e) Biggest risk
The role switcher erodes the security story: a tester reads "one account holds all five keys" as defeating separation of duties. It is honest only if we state the sandbox is a demo setup, keep a separate non-sandbox exam with real per-role logins that reviewers can attack, and log every switch. If we blur those two, the first attacker's finding is "your quorum is fake", and that is worse than having fewer roles.
