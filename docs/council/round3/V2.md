# V2 (validation), round 3: attack on the minimal system

## 1. Biggest hole: the tester cannot see P1, P2 or the fallback, and the Phase I report promised the fallback
A classmate logs in as `tester07`, which holds all four capabilities and all five key cards. They approve their own release. The security claim (P1, "no single person") is false in the only environment they use, and our video can only argue it was a demo artefact. Worse, "one submission per setter per topic" means one account puts one question per topic into the pool. The late random draw (P2) picks from a pool of one. The tester sees no randomness and no limit on a setter's exposure.

Then they open our Phase I report. R-S17 and R-F06 are **Must** ("fallback keeps the same controls", "built, to be tested") and the prototype shipped a fallback screen. The minimal design lists "resilience" as a given-up limit. That is a promised Must dropped. It sits in the 25% security satisfaction and in the Part B "requirements vs addressed" table.

## 2. Cut too far
- **Seeded setters in practice exams.** Each practice exam needs about 4 simulated setters, each with questions from the 100-question bank already in the repo. Without them P2 and the draw cannot be shown. Cost: 0.25 day in the seed script.
- **Fallback in minimal form.** Show 3-of-5 working with two holders absent and name it as the fallback, with absent holders and refusals logged. Make copies **idempotent per seat**: re-fetching seat k gives the same bytes and the same copy ID, and the cap counts distinct seats. That covers a printer jam, and a reprint then no longer breaks the cap. Cost: 0.25 day.
- **P6 evidence.** Add one line on the release page: "120 copies, same 20 questions, same total marks, set hash equal". Cost: 0.1 day. Phase I F4 and R-U11 depend on it.
- **R-S21, neighbouring seats never share an order (Must).** The code has no adjacency logic, and "seats 1..N" is linear. Enforce at least "adjacent seat numbers differ" and test it. Cost: 0.25 day.
- **R-S06 signed submissions and R-S08 reproducible draw.** Minimal drops both. At least record the seed in the log sealed (revealed after close) and an HMAC over each submission. Otherwise say plainly that these two Musts are design-only.

## 3. Not cut far enough
- 80 public tester accounts. Anyone can log in as anyone and wreck their exam, and "re-run seed on request" makes us a help desk. Allot by roll number and offer a self-reset link in the guide, or cut to about 40. Mapped to the course list, collisions drop.
- Five-card claim for a lone tester is heavy (Phase I: steps feel heavy). Practice exams should need only 3 cards, or auto-store cards in localStorage.
- G3 seat count is self-attested by the exam cell, who also sets the limit. Name it a cap, not a check.

## 4. First five minutes (the tester's view)
Login page, then Home. It must show one "Next step" button and the stage name. The guide must offer a 5-minute path: seal, 3 approvals, blocked-window screen, trace. If Home looks like a table of capabilities, usability marks go.

## 5. Single hall
A classmate will not mind single-hall if the video states it once. They will mind a missing fallback.

## Verdict: ACCEPT WITH CHANGES
Ranked:
1. Seeded simulated setters plus a draw readout (0.25 day).
2. Fallback as demonstrable 3-of-5 tolerance plus idempotent reprint (0.25 day).
3. P6 hash line (0.1 day).
4. Adjacent-seat rule (0.25 day).
5. Seed log plus HMAC, or label R-S06 and R-S08 design-only (0.2 day).
6. Tester allocation by roll number, with a self-reset link (0.2 day).
7. Home "next step" and a 5-minute path in the guide (0.25 day).

Total about 1.5 days, equal to the claimed saving. Hold the schedule by cutting the trace order parameter UI.
