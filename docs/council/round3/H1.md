# H1 (HCI / usable security): round 3

## 1. Biggest hole: the key card is two secrets, shown once, never rehearsed
A tired key holder must claim an encrypted text blob exactly once, keep it somewhere, and months later paste it on a phone and type a passphrase. Scenario: a holder closes the tab before copying, or pastes the card into a note and forgets the passphrase. Share gone, no recovery (the key was split at Seal). Two such holders are tolerated; a third and the exam is unprintable on the demo day, and we find out only at release. Phase I got 53% on the locked-paper check and never tested a custodian (R-U01). The passphrase adds nothing the account login does not already give: card plus login is two factors.

## 2. Cut too far
- **Card rehearsal.** The lost-card path was cut and nothing replaced it. Needed: P5.
- **Refuse.** Approve with no equal Refuse teaches rubber-stamping. Make it one decision field on the approve route, logged.
- **Notification.** With email and live updates cut, a key holder never learns a request exists, and 15 minutes pass. Home needs a "Waiting for you" row; the Release page needs a 5-second refresh.
- **Candidate note (F4, R-U11, Must).** No screen says "same questions, same marks". One line on every printed copy and in Help.

## 3. Not cut far enough
- **The passphrase and the encrypted blob.** Replace with a card of 12 words (the 127-bit share fits), printable and storable like a physical card. Removes the KDF, the browser crypto and the mistyped-passphrase failure. The server holds the plaintext share between Seal and claim in both designs, so exposure is unchanged.
- **Two-word check "compared by eye"** is passive. Make the key holder **type** the words, shown only on the exam cell panel.
- **One Release page for both audiences:** keep one route, but render two clearly separate panels. A tester who holds both must not see the words in the holder panel, or the check is theatre.

## 4. Lone tester, five cards, one account
This contradicts the "one share per account" rule, and "three different key holders" must count cards in practice. A tester would do five claims plus three paste-and-approve rounds alone, then see a production flow with one card and one approval. Practice must still make them paste at least once. Make the practice exam auto-issue cards with a visible PRACTICE banner and a card picker on the Release page.

## 5. Three kinds of people vs 25 interviewees
It matches. They followed a flow with named officials (Dean, Registrar) and an exam-cell persona; "capabilities" is invisible to users and fine, as long as the UI never says it and always names the role. Add one sentence on 3-of-5 on the Release page.

## 6. Verdict: ACCEPT WITH CHANGES
| Rank | Change | Days |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Verify-on-claim: card is re-shown until the holder pastes it back; exam page shows "cards verified n of 5"; release request locked below 4 | 0.5 |
| 2 | 12-word card, drop passphrase (user decides) | -0.25 |
| 3 | Typed two-word check plus Refuse | 0.25 |
| 4 | "Waiting for you" row and auto-refresh | 0.25 |
| 5 | Practice card picker, PRACTICE banner, G1 counts cards | 0.25 |
| 6 | Candidate note on printed copy and Help | 0.25 |

Net about +1.5 days, eating the saving. Test the claim and approve with 3 strangers on 16 Nov.
