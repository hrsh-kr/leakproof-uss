# S2 Red-team, round 3: attack on the minimal system

## 1. Biggest hole: public credentials in the same instance as the official exam
The package goes to the whole class, so the `tester01..80` passphrases are public. They share one DB, one process and one global log with the demo exam.
- A classmate logs in as tester17 and claims its five cards (the claim is once-only). The real tester17 is locked out of their own exam for good. Two classmates who both "take" tester17 do the same by accident, because nothing records who took what.
- The gate message ("Waiting for: Dean, Registrar") and the public log reveal the official usernames. Five bad logins on `dean` trips the SR2 lockout and the 26 Nov demo dies.
- Every tester holds `hold` and `print` on some exam. Routes that take an object id (`/releases/:id/*`, `/questions/:id`) and check "has the capability" without resolving the exam through that object become an IDOR open to 80 accounts. Sequential ids make the demo release trivial to enumerate.
- Any secret shipped in the package (cookie secret, copy-ID HMAC key, seeded DB) forges sessions on the live VM.

## 2. Cut too far
- **External pin for the log head.** The head hash on the same server proves nothing: whoever edits the DB recomputes the chain. P4 is empty without one outside copy per stage, plus an in-page verifier.
- **Sessions table.** None in the six tables, and the approvals held in RAM vanish on a crash. Any unhandled exception in the single Node process kills every tester at once.
- **Key card re-claim.** "Once" plus a failed clipboard copy loses a share forever. Two lost and one absent means a dead exam.
- **Seat count is immutable.** A typo means a fresh quorum in the hall. "Works once" must apply to approvals, not to fetching copies (a refresh must not destroy the exam).
- **Setup routes.** There is no assign route, and `POST /exams` cannot be exam-scoped, so any tester creates unlimited exams.
- **Seal precondition.** Fewer than 2 candidates per topic makes P2 void.
- **Solo practice.** One tester holds all five cards and never meets the multi-person ceremony, which is the product. Five claims plus three pastes alone is also the "too many steps" finding again.

## 3. Not cut far enough
- CSRF token endpoint: replace with an Origin check, required JSON content-type and a custom header. SameSite alone fails against sibling ports on the same VM host.
- Hard lockout: use delays.
- Practice exams: 2-of-3 instead of 3-of-5.
- Two-word check: it only means something if the approve body carries the nonce. Otherwise a static share replays across requests.

## 4. Verdict: ACCEPT WITH CHANGES
1. Two processes, two DBs, two secret sets. The demo does not run 18-25 Nov. Ids are random UUIDs, no-capability returns 404, and the package holds zero secrets (generate at first boot). **0.3d**
2. Replace the shipped list with a rate-limited, capped self-register that creates account and practice exam together and shows a generated passphrase once. Same seed function. **0.3d** (your call)
3. One exam resolver for every `:id` route, plus a test of each route against an account from another exam. **0.3d**
4. Backoff, sessions in SQLite, a systemd restart, log append in one transaction, a paginated public log with hashes only (no names, no copy IDs). **0.4d**
5. Post the head hash outside at each stage, with a verifier. **0.2d**
6. Re-claim until the first approval (logged), repeatable copy fetch while RELEASED, print by seat range up to the limit. **0.3d**
7. Seal needs at least 2 per topic, and practice uses 2-of-3. **0.1d**

Total about 1.9d against the 1-1.5d saved.
